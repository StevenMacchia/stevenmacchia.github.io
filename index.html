  1. Oct 16, 2026

    Every public safety claim is something someone can test

    State attorneys general are testing whether what platforms told parents about safety matches what happened, as in Florida's new consumer protection suit against Discord. Give one person the list of public safety claims, attach the evidence behind each, be exact about age checks, and recheck the list whenever the product changes.

  2. Oct 15, 2026

    A good Trust & Safety program is invisible

    When Trust & Safety works, neither users nor the company get hurt, so nobody sees it and leadership finds it easy to devalue and cut. Make it visible by measuring what didn't happen: exposure to harm, repeat offenders, time to action on the worst cases, and what users who ran into harm do next.

    Discuss on LinkedIn

  3. Oct 13, 2026

    Parents' top gaming concern is the rule they set least

    Ofcom's Growing Up Gaming research: 55% of children who game online play with people they've never met offline at least sometimes, and strangers are parents' top concern, yet only 43% set rules about who their child plays with. Platforms should close that gap with defaults (contact limited to people a child knows, friction on adults reaching unknown children, one clear parental switch) and measure how often strangers reach kids.

    Discuss on LinkedIn

  4. Oct 8, 2026

    What I'd tell myself in year one of Trust & Safety

    Five lessons from more than ten years in the field: treat safety as a systems problem built into the product, don't treat a ban as a closed case, measure what users experience rather than how busy the team was, let automation earn its scope, and give every risk one named owner.
